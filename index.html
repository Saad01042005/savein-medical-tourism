01 · Overview

What this project was about

SaveIN.Life is an early-stage startup looking to enter India's fragmented medical tourism facilitation market. Our team at 180 Degrees Consulting, NSUT was engaged to build the strategic foundation for their market entry — from understanding the competitive landscape to designing a viable, differentiated business model.

The Indian medical tourism market is unique: no single player holds dominance, the regulatory environment is evolving rapidly, and patient expectations are shifting toward end-to-end digital experiences. This presented both an opportunity and a challenge.

The core question we answered: How should SaveIN.Life position itself, what services should it prioritise, and how does it build a defensible business model in a 56% failure-rate market?

Our research spanned primary analysis of 10+ national MVTFs, 5 international players, India's legal and accreditation landscape, government schemes, and customer acquisition strategies — synthesised into a clear, actionable business blueprint.

Key Insight

The market has no dominant player. The opportunity window is now.

No MVTF holds more than ~5% market share. The top 3 funded players (HealthTrip, Vaidam, Health Konnect) collectively serve under 15% of annual inbound medical tourists. For a tech-first, patient-centric entrant with strong unit economics, this fragmentation is an advantage.

03 · Key Findings

What the research revealed

Competitive Landscape

The market is wide open — but brutal for the unprepared

Of 94 MVTFs tracked, 56% have been deadpooled. The average lifespan of failed MVTFs is 9 years — suggesting many entered without sustainable models, not that the market is inherently unviable.

  • Funded startups average 9.7 years of operation vs 7.1 for unfunded
  • Primary packages (travel + accommodation + treatment) are the core revenue driver across all top players
  • Only 2 of top 10 national MVTFs have a mobile app — significant gap
  • Patient reviews & testimonials are the #1 marketing channel used
Strategic Recommendation

Three things SaveIN.Life must get right from day one

Based on failure patterns and competitor gaps, survival and scale depend on getting these three fundamentals right before anything else.

  • Dedicated Patient Manager — almost no competitor offers this as a USP; it's the clearest trust signal for international patients
  • Transparent pricing & free first consultation — drives top-of-funnel conversion where most MVTFs fall short
  • Commission-first model over direct charging — reduces patient friction while building hospital relationships
Regulatory Environment

The government is actively building tailwinds for MVTFs

India's "Heal in India" initiative, e-Medical Visa expansion to 160+ countries, and the NABH MVTF empanelment programme all signal strong government intent to formalise and grow this sector.

  • NABH MVTF empanelment launched in 2016 — only a handful of players have it, making it a strong differentiator
  • Ministry of Tourism offers up to ₹25 lakh in financial support for qualifying medical tourism promotions
  • NABH accreditation likely to become mandatory — early compliance is a moat
Customer Insight

68% of patients are from Bangladesh — the target segment is closer than it seems

The dominant inbound markets are neighbouring countries, not Western nations. This shapes language, logistics, and marketing strategy significantly.

  • Bangladesh, Maldives, Myanmar, Oman & Yemen are top source markets
  • 40–60 age group dominates for cardiac & orthopaedic procedures
  • 18–30 age cohort is the fastest-growing segment (fertility, dental, cosmetic)
  • Email + WhatsApp are the dominant patient communication channels

06 · Results & Reflection

What happened next, and what I learned

SaveIN.Life used this research to inform their immediate product and go-to-market decisions. The prioritised service framework [1/2/3] became the backbone of their MVP roadmap, guiding which features to build first vs defer. The competitive analysis — particularly the finding that only 2 of 10 top national MVTFs had a mobile app — directly shaped their decision to invest early in a patient-facing app. And the regulatory mapping enabled them to plan for NABH empanelment from day one, turning compliance into a competitive moat rather than an afterthought.

The most impactful insight was quantitative: no MVTF holds more than ~5% market share. This fundamentally reframed the strategy from "how do we compete with established giants?" to "how do we move faster, smarter, and with better unit economics than 94 fragmented competitors?" The entire strategic posture shifted. Instead of building defensibility through scale, we focused on defensibility through differentiation (the dedicated patient manager) and capital efficiency (commission-first revenue model).

The research also validated an underrated insight: patient communication is still dominated by email and WhatsApp, not fancy SaaS dashboards. This led SaveIN.Life to invest in WhatsApp automation and transactional email before building complex internal tools — a disciplined approach to MVP sequencing that most healthtech startups get wrong.

What I'd do differently

Research Depth

Customer interviews would have come earlier

We relied heavily on secondary research and competitive analysis. This gave us a solid market map, but we missed nuance on patient pain points that could have shaped the business model more sharply. In a follow-up phase, I'd conduct 15-20 interviews with actual medical tourists from Bangladesh and neighbouring countries before finalising the service roadmap.

Unit Economics

Revenue streams needed deeper financial modelling

We identified 7 revenue streams, but didn't deeply model the unit economics of each. What's the actual commission % from hospitals? How does patient LTV compare to CAC across different acquisition channels? A financial model showing 3-year projections under different scenarios would have made the recommendations more actionable for fundraising.

Stakeholder Feedback Loop

Testing recommendations with real users earlier

All our findings were internally validated but not tested against actual hospitals or patient cohorts. A mid-stage workshop with key hospital partners and a sample of target patients would have pressure-tested the business model and caught blind spots. Consulting findings are only as good as their real-world applicability.

Execution Roadmap

More granular phase-gating and milestones

The [1/2/3] prioritisation was clear, but the business model would have been stronger with specific go/no-go criteria for each phase: "After 90 days of MVP, we'll measure X. If we don't see Y, we pivot to Z." This kind of decision-making clarity is what separates strategy from wishful thinking.

Frameworks that transferred

The research methodology and strategic frameworks developed for medical tourism proved directly applicable to other B2B healthcare and SaaS markets. The market fragmentation analysis, competitive benchmarking structure, regulatory mapping, and revenue model canvas became reusable templates. The key insight — that a fragmented market with high failure rates is an opportunity, not a liability — applies to any emerging category where execution matters more than innovation.
